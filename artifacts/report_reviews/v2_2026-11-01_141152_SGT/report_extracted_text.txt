Abstract
This study forecasts same-session open-to-close returns for ten fixed current Nifty IT constituents from information aligned to the 08:55 IST decision clock. The canonical depth-constrained Extra Trees model combines own-stock, peer, U.S. IT-services, broad-market, macro and dated AI context, while current-vintage macro fallbacks remain an identified limitation. The signed strategy applies a 15-bps effective round-trip hurdle and modeled turnover costs. In the 2025 historical replication it earns -4.04% net with 12.86% annualized volatility, Sharpe -0.26, and OOS R² versus the asset mean of -0.75%. In the separate January–September 2026 retrospective extension it earns 15.01%, with 13.74% volatility, Sharpe 1.46, and positive mean IC. The combined 10.37% path is dominated by the declining 2026 segment. The pooled AI-release effect is inconclusive, while the 2026 coefficient is negative after multiplicity adjustment; neither result identifies causality. The evidence supports a conditional defensive-timing hypothesis, not robust stock-selection alpha.

1. Research question and hypotheses
The primary question is whether completed U.S. and global information improves the decision to take a long, short, or flat position in current Nifty IT constituents at the Indian open. The secondary question is whether frontier-model releases or capability improvements contain incremental information about the subsequent returns of U.S. IT-services and Indian IT-services firms.
Six evidence questions organize the final report. H1–H6 below follow the saved hypothesis-analysis protocol; they replace the earlier draft numbering. Peer information and feature complexity remain controlled modeling comparisons in Sections 4–5. These questions were refined after inspecting historical results and are exploratory, not prospectively preregistered tests.

1.1 Relation to the assigned research
Chinco, Clark-Joseph, and Ye (2019) motivate many-stock, short-lived predictors with explicit complexity penalties. This study implements sparse and shrinkage baselines, training-only feature selection, and frequent temporal refits. Kelly, Malamud, and Zhou (2024) motivate the possibility that a large feature space improves market timing when complexity is tuned carefully. Here, complexity is tested through nested information sets, PCA, regularized linear models, boosting, and constrained tree ensembles; increasing dimension is treated as an empirical hypothesis rather than a guaranteed benefit. The active signed rule uses 5 bps fee/spread proxy plus 2.5 bps slippage each way. The effective threshold is therefore max(5 bps configured forecast floor, 15 bps round-trip friction) = 15 bps. Up to three forecasts above +15 bps are long and up to three below −15 bps are short. Absolute stock weight is capped near one-third and total gross exposure at one. Positions enter from cash at the vendor open, exit at the close, and incur two-sided turnover equal to twice gross exposure. The +1/0/−1 states describe direction, not literal unit weights.

7. Hypothesis evidence and regime diagnostics
7.1 Test definitions and multiplicity
The saved analysis aggregates observations by trading date rather than treating ten correlated stock rows as independent replications. H1 regresses Indian cohort intraday return on the available U.S. services return, lagged Indian return and lagged broad-market return. H2 replaces the U.S. term with the release indicator to avoid conditioning on a possible transmission channel. Newey–West HAC covariance uses five lags [19]. All estimable regression p-values in the saved family receive Holm step-down correction [20]; displayed confidence intervals are pointwise, not simultaneous. Circular date-block resampling uses 2,000 draws for H5. These controls do not erase prior researcher exposure to the sample.

Because both return variables are in decimal units, the pooled H1 coefficient implies approximately +0.098 percentage points of Indian intraday return per +1 percentage point of the available U.S. return, conditional on the included lags. The 2026 coefficient is 0.181 (Holm p=0.0240), but the post-2024 change interaction is inconclusive (Holm p=1.000). A significant coefficient in one year and an insignificant coefficient in another is not itself a significant difference between years.
The pooled 2023–2026 H2 estimate is −0.169 percentage points with an interval spanning zero. In 2026 alone, 22 marked sessions yield −0.695 percentage points (95% HAC interval −1.099 to −0.292; Holm p=0.0155). That year-specific association is exploratory because the calendar is retrospective, events are sparse, and no market-adjusted causal design is claimed. H3 does not show attenuation after 2024: its interaction is negative but does not survive multiplicity adjustment. H4 is withheld under its sample-size rule rather than promoted from the small capability sample.

7.5 H6: adaptation, staffing and credible counterevidence
Commercial adaptation is observable without assuming that AI necessarily improves margins. Infosys announced its Topaz generative-AI services offering on 23 May 2023 [9]. The Bank of England/FCA 2024 survey reports human oversight in semi-autonomous financial-services applications and only 2% of use cases with fully autonomous decision-making [10]. These sources support the relevance of enterprise integration and governance. They do not establish that Indian clients retained particular vendors because of cybersecurity, or that AI caused an equity recovery.
CX Dive reported on 9 May 2025 that Klarna was piloting recruitment of human customer-service staff while retaining its chatbot [11]. This is evidence of a hybrid support arrangement, not proof that the company abandoned AI or rehired a specified number of software developers. Conversely, Reuters reported renewed Indian IT disruption concerns on 5 February 2026, alongside analysts who considered the selloff excessive [12]. This counterexample prevents a one-directional narrative of early fear followed by universal acceptance.

8.2 Limitations
The fixed current-member cohort creates survivorship conditioning. Public vendor opens abstract from opening-auction queueing, bid–ask spread variation, taxes, borrow availability, short-sale constraints, market impact, and corporate-action edge cases. S&P and Nifty membership histories are public reconstructions rather than licensed exchange archives. Macro releases may require conservative lags, and some current-vintage fallbacks are not fully point-in-time.
The effective inference sample is the number of serially dependent trading days, not the number of stock-day rows. AI releases are few, parameter disclosure is selective, parameter counts are not comparable across dense and mixture-of-experts architectures, and benchmark progress is multidimensional. The 2026 extension occurs during a sharp sector drawdown; its official-provider release calendar was reconstructed retrospectively through 30 September rather than maintained prospectively.
8.3 Conclusion
The strongest contribution is a temporally aligned, auditable retrospective design that converts cross-market forecasts into signed positions and reconciled costs. Extra Trees ranks highest under the configured historical confirmation rule, but the 2025 replication has weak forecast and net economic evidence: R² versus the asset mean and mean IC are negative, and costs more than eliminate the 2025 gross gain, taking +5.16% gross to −4.04% net. The 2026 extension is strongly positive during a sharp sector decline, making the combined result promising but regime-sensitive and dominated by defensive timing. Pooled AI-event evidence remains inconclusive; the negative 2026 association is exploratory rather than causal. The next evidentiary steps are strict-vintage sensitivity, paired timing-versus-selection inference and genuinely future observations after a prospective freeze.
